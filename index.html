Case study · AI systems design

A failing gate blocks the build. There is no partial pass.

Most AI systems produce an answer and hope it's right. This one is built so that it cannot produce an answer it can't defend — and stops instead. Below is the actual flow, running a $15M+ engineering programme. Trip a gate and watch what happens downstream.

Defensible

Gate 01 · Evidence

Nothing is produced from a summary

No artefact may be produced until the source documents behind it have been opened and a retrieval manifest written — not the index, the source. The extracted layer is a pointer to the documents, never a substitute for them.

Coverage is proven rather than asserted: any blank cell in the proof is a stop, not a footnote. An artefact produced without a manifest is void, and is regenerated rather than patched.

Origin: written after a plan assembled from database summaries omitted three passenger lifts and a 1500 kVA generator — two of the three longest-lead items on the project. A four-minute sweep of the source found all of it. None of it required judgement; all of it required reading the source.

The rules

Four constraints that bind every reply

Not guidelines. Each one exists because something went wrong once, and each is enforced mechanically rather than remembered.

RULE 01

No output without a source

Every statement traces to a document, a clause, or a numbered decision. A citation against a document that was not read in full is void — which is what catches a reference typed from memory.

RULE 02

No number from memory

Every count, total and inventory is produced by a verification script in the current session, or it is not stated. A figure carried across a summary is hearsay, and a non-zero exit blocks the build.

RULE 03

No negative on one axis

Nothing is reported missing, absent, unpriced or wrong until it is proven so on two independent tests, both named in the reply. If the second test can't run, the finding is unverified — not counted, not totalled.

RULE 04

Stopping is a valid answer

Where the evidence isn't there, the system halts and says which file it needs. It does not produce something plausible to fill the gap. Refusal is a designed output, not a failure.

What it caught

Two incidents, in opposite directions

The first is the error everyone expects AI to make. The second is the one almost nobody designs for — and it is the more expensive of the two.

~$490,000

of scope that wasn't in the plan

A work breakdown built from database summaries looked complete. It had omitted three passenger lifts and a 1500 kVA generator with a 72-hour fuel tank — two of the three longest-lead items on the programme.

Nothing about finding them required expertise. It required opening the priced bill at item level and sweeping the drawing register by filename, which is now a gate rather than a habit.

Result → the evidence gate, and a coverage proof where a blank cell stops the build

~$604,000

that was never actually missing

An automated findings register reported 18 packages with no supporting chain and $604,000 unaccounted for, and proposed creating seven new chains. Every check in place passed. The coverage meter read 100%.

Re-tested by name instead of by reference number, every one of the 18 already had a full chain. Their references were simply wrong — and a reference test cannot find a wrong reference. The fix was 45 corrections, not 34 inventions.

Result → the two-axis rule, plus a screen that itself flagged 242 correct rows before being corrected

Why this matters outside engineering

The domain is engineering. The architecture isn't.

Every business deploying AI on its own documents faces the same two failures. The output that invents something plausible, and the output that confidently reports a problem which does not exist. The second is rarer, more expensive, and almost never designed against — because it passes every test you thought to write.

The layers that stop both are not industry-specific: grounding every claim in a named source, computing figures rather than recalling them, proving a negative twice before stating it, and designing a refusal path so the system stops instead of guessing.

What changes between industries is the vocabulary. A contract, a bill of quantities and a drawing register become a policy, a price list and a case file. The gates stay exactly where they are.
